# Social Media Copywriting

Craft for writing social media captions and post copy that performs — specifically for Instagram, extensible to other platforms.

## Caption Anatomy

A high-performing Instagram caption has three parts:

### 1. The Open (first line)
This is the only text visible before "...more". It must earn the tap.

**What works:**
- An observation: "Every business owner I've met this year says the same thing about their website."
- A statement that creates a gap: "The best marketing campaign I ever ran cost $0."
- A counterintuitive claim: "Your brand doesn't need a rebrand."
- A specific detail: "43 seconds. That's how long the average person looks at a website before deciding."

**What doesn't:**
- Questions ("Want to know the secret to...?") — overused, feels like bait
- Proclamations ("I'm so excited to announce...") — nobody cares about your excitement
- Generic hooks ("In today's competitive market...") — instant scroll-past
- Listicle previews ("5 tips for better marketing") — commoditised format, gets saved but not read

### 2. The Body (middle)
Deliver on the open's promise. One idea, explored.

**Structure options:**
- **Story:** a specific moment → what happened → what it means
- **Argument:** claim → evidence → implication
- **List:** 3-5 concrete points (not generic tips — specific, actionable)
- **Observation:** describe what you noticed → why it matters

**Length:**
- Short (< 100 chars): quote posts, punchy observations
- Medium (100-300 chars): most posts. Enough to say something, short enough to read
- Long (300-500 chars): deep dives, stories, educational content. Only when the content earns it.
- Very long (500+): rarely. Carousels are better for long-form — put it in the slides, not the caption.

### 3. The Close
Not a CTA (usually). A thought to leave with.

**What works:**
- An earned conclusion: "So yeah. Maybe the rebrand isn't the problem."
- A gentle redirect: "Link in bio if you want to see what we did about it."
- A conversation starter: "What's the worst marketing advice you've ever taken?"
- Nothing: sometimes the body IS the ending. Don't add a closer just because there's a convention.

**What doesn't:**
- "Like and share if you agree!" — engagement bait, algorithm penalises it
- "DM me 'GROWTH' for my free guide" — sales funnel garbage
- "Follow for more content like this" — desperate
- A hashtag wall — see below

## Hashtag Strategy (2025-2026)

Hashtags are functionally dead for discovery on Instagram. The algorithm serves content based on interest signals, not hashtag follows.

**Current best practice:**
- 0-2 hashtags maximum
- Brand tag only if you have one (`#superbadmarketing`)
- Place in caption body where they read naturally, not as a block at the end
- Never use trending/generic hashtags (#marketing #business #entrepreneur)
- If a hashtag doesn't add context, it's clutter

## Caption Voice Adaptation

The SuperBad brand voice (dry, observational, real) adapts slightly per content type:

### Portfolio / Showcase
Tone: confident but not bragging. Let the work speak.
```
Bad: "So proud of this incredible transformation! Our team knocked it out of the park!"
Good: "Before: a website from 2019. After: a website from 2026. Took four weeks and three espressos."
```

### Observation / Authority
Tone: casual expertise. You know things because you've done things, not because you read things.
```
Bad: "Top 5 marketing trends you need to know in 2026!"
Good: "Everyone's talking about AI-generated content. Nobody's talking about how obvious AI-generated content looks."
```

### Anti-Motivation / Real Talk
Tone: honest, slightly tired, fundamentally hopeful underneath.
```
Bad: "Hustle hard! Every setback is a setup for a comeback! #grindset"
Good: "Day 847 of running a business. Still haven't figured out invoicing. The marketing's good though."
```

### Client Stories / Social Proof
Tone: specific, results-focused, understated.
```
Bad: "We're thrilled to have helped [Client] achieve AMAZING results!!!"
Good: "Six months ago, [Client] had a logo and a prayer. Now they've got a wait list. Here's what changed."
```

## Platform-Specific Conventions

### Instagram Feed
- First line is the hook (preview text)
- Line breaks matter — use them for pacing
- @mentions in caption boost collaborative reach
- Location tags add local discoverability (still works, unlike hashtags)

### Instagram Stories
- Text overlay: 2-4 words max per text block
- Use native Instagram text tools for authenticity
- Polls and questions drive engagement (but don't overuse — fatigue sets in)
- Story captions should feel impromptu, even when planned

### Instagram Reels
- Caption is secondary to video content
- Keep caption under 100 chars — most people don't read reel captions
- Use caption for context the video doesn't provide, not to repeat the video

## Writing for Saves

Posts get saved when they contain reference value — something the user wants to come back to.

**Save triggers:**
- Actionable frameworks or checklists
- Specific examples or case studies
- Data or statistics worth remembering
- Templates or formulas
- Before/after comparisons with clear lessons

**How to write for saves:**
- Be specific, not generic. "Post at 7am on Tuesday" saves. "Post at the right time" doesn't.
- Structure the information so it's scannable on re-read
- Make the value obvious — if someone saves it, what will they use it for?

## Writing for Shares

Posts get shared when they make the sharer look good — smart, funny, or ahead of the curve.

**Share triggers:**
- Takes that feel true but unsaid ("Finally someone said it")
- Industry observations that make the sharer look informed
- Humour that lands (not broad — niche enough to feel like an in-joke)
- Contrarian views backed by evidence

**How to write for shares:**
- Write the thing people think but don't say
- Be concise — shared posts need to be understood without context
- Make the sharer look good by association

## Common Copywriting Mistakes on Social

1. **Writing for yourself, not the audience.** Nobody cares about your journey — they care about what your journey means for them.
2. **Being clever over being clear.** If the wordplay obscures the point, cut it.
3. **Burying the lead.** The best line in your caption shouldn't be line 7.
4. **Over-explaining.** Trust the reader. If the image shows a transformation, you don't need to say "as you can see, this is a transformation."
5. **Consistent monotone.** Vary rhythm, tone, and structure between posts. The same cadence every time becomes wallpaper.
6. **Writing captions in isolation.** The caption + image is one unit. Don't repeat what the image already shows.
