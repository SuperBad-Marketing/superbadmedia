# Social Proof Content

How to turn client results, testimonials, and case studies into social media content that builds credibility and drives enquiries.

## Why Social Proof Converts

People trust evidence over claims. "We make great marketing" means nothing. "This clinic's bookings went up 300% in 6 months" means everything. Social proof bridges the gap between what you say you do and what you actually do.

On Instagram specifically, social proof content:
- Gets saved (people bookmark it as evidence when pitching your services internally)
- Gets shared (clients share posts about themselves; prospects share posts about impressive results)
- Gets screenshot-DM'd to business partners ("we should talk to these people")
- Converts profile visitors to followers (the grid becomes a portfolio of evidence)

## Types of Social Proof

### 1. Transformation Posts
Before/after or journey posts that show measurable change.

**Structure:**
- Slide 1: the hook — the headline result ("From 12 enquiries a month to 47. Here's what changed.")
- Slide 2: the before — context on where they started (specific, not generic)
- Slides 3-N: what was done — the specific actions, not vague "strategy"
- Final slide: the after — the result with numbers

**Caption approach:**
Open with the result, then tell the story. End with what it means, not "DM us for the same."

**Compliance note for medical aesthetics:** Never show clinical before/after images on Instagram. Show the brand transformation — website, social presence, patient experience — not the clinical outcomes. TGA and AHPRA restrictions apply.

### 2. Testimonial Posts
Direct quotes from clients, presented as designed graphics.

**Formats:**
- Single slide: the quote + client name/business + a tasteful headshot or logo
- Carousel: 3-5 testimonials from different clients, one per slide
- Story: screenshot-style testimonial (feels authentic, informal)

**Design rules:**
- Large quotation marks are dated. Let the typography carry it.
- Client photo or logo adds credibility. A quote from "Business Owner, Melbourne" is worth less than "Sarah Chen, Founder of Revive Aesthetics."
- Keep the quote to 2-3 sentences. Edit for impact (with permission).

**Caption approach:**
Don't just repeat the quote. Add context: what the challenge was, what you did, why the quote matters.

### 3. Metric Posts
A single impressive number, designed as a hero graphic.

**Examples:**
- "4,200% ROI on a $2,000 campaign"
- "12 → 47 enquiries per month"
- "3 days from brief to launch"

**Design:**
- The number is the focal point — huge, bold, impossible to scroll past
- Supporting context in smaller text
- Brand colours, minimal design. The number IS the design.

**Caption approach:**
Tell the story behind the number. Numbers without narrative are hollow.

### 4. Process Posts
Show how you work. Builds trust through transparency.

**Formats:**
- Carousel: step-by-step breakdown of a project (5-8 slides)
- Reel: timelapse or walkthrough of the creative process
- Single image: a tool, workspace, or mid-process screenshot

**Why it works:**
People buy processes they understand. "We do great work" is unverifiable. "Here's exactly how we approach a brand refresh, step by step" is reassuring.

### 5. Named Reference Posts
Posts that name specific clients and their specific results — with permission.

**Why named > anonymous:**
- "A dental clinic in Melbourne" = generic, forgettable
- "Smile Studio, Richmond" = verifiable, credible, shareable (they'll share it too)

**Permission framework:**
- Always get written permission before posting client-identifying content
- Offer the client a preview before it goes live
- Tag them in the post — collaborative reach + they feel valued
- If they decline naming, respect it and use the anonymous format

## The Social Proof Flywheel

Social proof compounds:

```
Good work → Client result → Social proof post → New prospect sees it →
Prospect enquires → Good work → Client result → Social proof post → ...
```

### Accelerating the Flywheel
1. **Ask for testimonials at the right moment** — right after a milestone (launch day, first month results, 6-month review). Not in a generic email weeks later.
2. **Make it easy** — send them 3 specific prompts to respond to, not "could you write us a review?"
3. **Capture results in real-time** — screenshot dashboards, save analytics reports, photograph reaction moments. You can't go back and capture these later.
4. **Build social proof into the service delivery** — if you're going to post about results, set up tracking from day 1.

## Vertical-Specific Social Proof

### Medical Aesthetics
- **Show:** brand evolution, social media growth, website before/after, patient volume growth (numbers only, no patient content)
- **Don't show:** clinical before/after, patient faces (without consent + TGA compliance), specific treatment outcomes
- **Angle:** "We made this clinic impossible to ignore" not "Look at these results"

### Financial Planning
- **Show:** client acquisition numbers, brand positioning, content performance, event attendance
- **Don't show:** specific financial returns, individual client financial data, compliance-sensitive advice
- **Angle:** "We helped this adviser become the obvious choice" not "Their clients made X%"

### Allied Health
- **Show:** booking growth, brand awareness metrics, referral increases, community engagement
- **Don't show:** patient outcomes, specific health improvements, treatment details
- **Angle:** "This practice went from hidden gem to fully booked" not "Patients got better"

## Frequency & Mix

Social proof should be ~15% of total content mix (per the content pillar strategy). Within that 15%:

- 40% transformation/case study posts (the heavyweight proof)
- 30% testimonial posts (quick, easy, high-trust)
- 20% metric posts (punchy, shareable)
- 10% process/BTS posts (trust-building)

Don't cluster social proof posts together. Space them out — one every 5-7 posts in the grid. Too much social proof feels like a sales page, not a feed.
