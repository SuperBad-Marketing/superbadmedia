Page 2 / Identity
01 · Identity
“A quiet stubbornness dressed in work boots. They don’t explain why the coffee’s good. They just make it good and let people figure it out.”
FROM YOUR BRAND DNA

Groundwork Coffee is built on the idea that doing things properly isn’t a marketing angle. It’s just how you work. The founders came from hospitality backgrounds where the product spoke first and the branding caught up later, and that instinct runs through everything they do.

Their DNA reads like someone who’d rather show you the roasting process than write a tagline about it. There’s a warmth here, but it’s the warmth of the kitchen, not the sales floor. Texture matters. Imperfection is tolerated, maybe even preferred, when it comes with honesty attached.

The strongest signal across all five sections was a pull toward craft without preciousness. They want to be taken seriously without taking themselves too seriously. That tension is the brand.

Page 5 / Content Pillars
04 · Content
Content Pillars

The recurring themes that anchor your content strategy. Every post, article, or campaign should trace back to one of these.

01 The Process

Behind-the-scenes of sourcing, roasting, and dialling in. Not tutorials. Not education. Just honest documentation of what it takes to get the thing right. The audience doesn’t need to understand extraction ratios. They need to see that someone cares enough to get them right.

02 Neighbourhood Fabric

Groundwork exists in a physical place with real regulars. Content that treats the neighbourhood as a character, not a backdrop. The barista who remembers orders. The tradie who comes in at 6am. The dog that sits outside every Tuesday. Stories that make people want to be there.

03 Quiet Opinions

Groundwork has strong views on quality, sourcing, and how coffee should be served. This pillar gives them permission to say those things out loud, without sounding preachy. One-liners, short captions, the occasional longer piece that explains why they don’t do oat milk (or why they do).

04 Maker’s Almanac

Seasonal shifts, new beans, menu changes, collaborations with other makers. Not announcements. More like dispatches from someone who notices things. “The Ethiopian Yirgacheffe is back. We’d say it’s our favourite, but we say that about all of them.”

05 Craft, Not Coffee

Cross-pollination with other crafts and makers. The furniture builder who made the tables. The ceramicist who threw the cups. The printer who did the menus. Groundwork’s brand extends beyond coffee into an appreciation for doing things by hand, properly.

Page 6 / Brand Voice
05 · Voice
Brand Voice Guide

Groundwork sounds like a mate who happens to know a lot about coffee but doesn’t lead with it. Conversational register, short sentences, dry humour that lands because it’s honest. Never performative. The energy is calm confidence, not enthusiasm.

DO
+ Write like you talk. If you wouldn’t say it across the counter, don’t post it.
+ Let the product speak. A close-up of latte art says more than a paragraph about your barista’s training.
+ Use real names. “Sarah pulled this one” beats “our talented team crafted.”
+ Be specific. “Washed process, stone fruit, brown sugar finish” not “amazing coffee.”
+ Admit when something didn’t work. “That batch wasn’t it. This one is.”
DON'T
× Corporate speak. “We’re passionate about delivering exceptional coffee experiences.” Nobody talks like that.
× Hashtag stuffing. Two maximum. #groundworkcoffee if any. That’s it.
× Exclamation marks. If the sentence needs one, the sentence isn’t good enough.
× Trend-chasing. Don’t post about National Coffee Day. Don’t do Reels because everyone else is.
× Humblebragging. “We’re so grateful for this incredible recognition” reads as insecure.
HOW YOU SOUND

“We changed the blend. You probably won’t notice. We will.”

“Open 6am. The espresso machine is ready before we are.”

“Someone asked if we do iced matcha lattes. We do now. It hurt a little.”

“The Yirgacheffe is back. Blueberry, jasmine, the usual argument about whether it tastes like tea.”
