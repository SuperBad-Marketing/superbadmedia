Why your competitor's blog ranks and yours collects dust.
superbadmedia.com.au/blog/competitor-blog-ranks · 1,840 words · 8 min read

You've got a blog. It's sitting there. Four posts from 2023 and a "Welcome to our new website" entry that aged like milk. Meanwhile your competitor — the one with the worse logo and the Wix site — is ranking for every search your customers are making.

That's not because they're better at marketing. It's because they're writing about the things people are actually searching for, and you're writing about what you think sounds impressive at a networking event.

The uncomfortable truth about content.

Most small business blogs fail for the same reason most gyms make money: the owner signs up with good intentions, writes two posts about "Our Approach to Quality," realises nobody's reading them, and quietly stops. The gym keeps charging. Your blog just sits there, silently judging you from the About page dropdown.

Content isn't about proving you know things. It's about showing up where your customer is already looking.

The difference between a blog that ranks and one that doesn't isn't writing quality — it's research. A 500-word post targeting a keyword with actual search volume and low competition will outperform a 3,000-word masterpiece about "synergistic growth strategies" every single time.

What "keyword research" actually means for a small business.

Not what the SEO agency charges you $2,000 a month to pretend to do. Real keyword research is asking: what are my customers typing into Google right now, and which of those searches doesn't already have a perfect answer from a massive website?

That's the gap. That's where a small business blog wins — not by outwriting Forbes, but by answering the specific question Forbes would never bother with.
