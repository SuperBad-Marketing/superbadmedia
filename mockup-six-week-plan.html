Your Six-Week Plan

You've got a brewery that looks the part and beer that speaks for itself — but right now, nobody outside your taproom regulars knows that. This plan is built around the footage we shot and the gaps in your current setup. Six weeks, one thing at a time. If you run it yourself, it works. If you want us to run it, it becomes your retainer strategy on day one.

Week 1 Lay the plumbing

Before you post anything, you need somewhere for interested people to go that isn't just your Instagram bio link. This week is about getting the boring foundations in place so that everything you do from Week 2 onwards actually captures attention instead of leaking it.

Content angles
Workspace hero shot of the brewhouse floor — use as the header image on your Google Business Profile and landing page
Owner portrait at the bar — LinkedIn profile update and "about" page, establishes you as the face behind the brand
Channels
Google Business Profile Email Website
Tasks
Claim and complete your Google Business Profile
Upload the brewhouse hero shot, add your hours, write a 2-sentence description that mentions craft beer + your suburb. This is how 70% of local search traffic finds you.
Infrastructure Half day
Set up an email capture on your website
Simple signup form — "First to know about new releases." No lead magnet needed yet, just start collecting addresses. Even 20 emails in Week 1 is a win.
Infrastructure Half day
Update your Instagram bio link
Point it to a simple link-in-bio page with: email signup, Google Maps directions, and your taproom hours. Takes 20 minutes with Linktree or similar.
Infrastructure Quick
Success signal
Google Business Profile is live, email form is capturing signups, and your Instagram bio links to something useful.
If not: Focus on Google Business Profile alone — it's the highest-leverage single task this week.
Week 2 Show them what you look like

You've got professional footage now. This week is about getting it in front of people who already follow you, and giving them a reason to share it. First impressions set the tone for everything that follows.

Content angles
Short-form video (the one we shot) — post natively on Instagram Reels and TikTok. Caption: something honest about what goes into a batch, not a sales pitch.
Detail shots of the taps and glassware — carousel post showing the textures and craft of your space. Let the photography do the talking.
Behind-the-scenes stills from shoot day — story sequence. People love seeing the process. Tag us if you want, don't if you don't.
Channels
Instagram Reels TikTok Instagram Stories
Tasks
Post the short-form video to Reels and TikTok
Post natively (upload to each platform separately, don't cross-post). Write a caption that sounds like you, not like marketing. 2-3 relevant hashtags max.
Content Quick
Share a 4-image carousel of the best stills
Pick your four strongest images. No text overlays. Let them breathe. Post on Instagram feed with a short caption about what makes your space yours.
Content Quick
Run a 3-frame Instagram Story from shoot day
Behind-the-scenes. Raw, not polished. Add a poll sticker on the last frame: "Which new beer should we feature next?" — easy engagement, useful data.
Distribution Quick
Success signal
The video gets at least 500 views in 48 hours and you gain 15+ new followers across platforms.
If not: Share the video to your personal profile and ask 5 friends to reshare. Organic reach needs a push to start.
Week 3 Start the conversation

You've posted, people have seen it — now make them feel like insiders. This week shifts from broadcasting to building a relationship with the people who showed up. Reviews and direct engagement are your highest-trust signals.

Content angles
Pour shot close-up — pair with a "what are you drinking this weekend?" prompt. Conversational, not polished.
Taproom atmosphere shot — use for a Google review request post. Show the vibe they're endorsing.
Channels
Instagram Feed Google Reviews Email
Tasks
Ask your top 10 regulars for a Google review
Send them the direct review link personally. Don't blast it — pick the people who genuinely love the place. 5 real reviews outweigh 50 generic ones.
Conversion Quick
Send your first email to everyone who signed up
Short, warm, not salesy. "Hey — thanks for signing up. Here's what's pouring this week." Include one photo from the shoot. That's it.
Distribution Half day
Reply to every comment and DM from last week
Not with emojis — with actual words. Ask a follow-up question. The algorithm rewards conversations, not reactions.
Distribution Quick
Success signal
You've added 5+ Google reviews and sent your first email with an open rate above 40%.
If not: Follow up with the reviewers in person next time they visit. Face-to-face asks convert 3x better than digital ones.
Week 4 Bring in new faces

You've built a foundation and warmed up your existing audience. Now it's time to reach people who don't know you exist yet. A small, targeted ad spend goes further than you think when the creative is already professional.

Content angles
Short-form video (repurpose from Week 2) — use as the primary ad creative. It already performed organically, which means the algorithm will back it with spend.
Taproom atmosphere shot — secondary ad creative for the "visit us" angle. Different hook, same quality bar.
Channels
Meta Ads Instagram Feed
Tasks
Set up a Meta ad campaign targeting 10km radius
$5-10/day budget. Target 25-45 year olds interested in craft beer, local food, or nightlife within 10km of your venue. Use the video as the primary creative.
Distribution Half day
Cross-promote with a nearby food venue
Pick one restaurant or food truck you respect. Propose a simple mutual shoutout — they post about your beer, you post about their food. No money changes hands.
Distribution Quick
Post a "new faces" welcome on Instagram
Acknowledge that new people are finding you. "If you just followed us — welcome. Here's what we're about." Use the owner portrait. Authentic, not corporate.
Content Quick
Success signal
Your ad reaches 5,000+ people and you see at least 3 new faces at the taproom who mention they found you online.
If not: Check your targeting radius — if it's too narrow, widen to 15km. If the creative isn't clicking, swap in the carousel images as a static ad.
Week 5 Give them a reason to come back

Acquiring a new customer costs 5x more than keeping an existing one. This week is about turning first-timers into regulars and giving your email list a reason to stay subscribed. An event or a release gives people a calendar-worthy reason to return.

Content angles
Remaining shoot stills you haven't used yet — save the best for an event announcement or new release tease. Fresh assets keep your feed from looking repetitive.
User-generated content from taproom visitors — reshare (with permission) alongside your professional shots. Shows social proof without you having to say "trust us."
Channels
Email Instagram Stories In-venue
Tasks
Plan and announce a taproom event
Doesn't have to be big — a tasting night, a food truck collab, a new release party. Pick a date 2-3 weeks out and announce via email + stories. Give people a reason to put it in their calendar.
Conversion Half day
Send your second email newsletter
Lead with the event. Include one behind-the-scenes photo. End with "reply to this email if you're coming" — replies boost deliverability and make people feel heard.
Distribution Half day
Put a QR code on your bar
Links to your email signup. Print it on a small card or sticker next to the taps. Every person sitting at your bar is already a warm lead — capture them.
Infrastructure Quick
Success signal
Your event has 20+ RSVPs or replies, and your email list has grown to 50+ subscribers.
If not: Promote the event on your personal networks. Founders sharing their own events gets 3x the engagement of brand accounts.
Week 6 Measure what worked and keep going

Most businesses do six weeks of marketing and then stop because they don't know if it worked. This week is about looking at the numbers, understanding what moved, and setting up a rhythm you can sustain without burning out. The goal isn't perfection — it's momentum.

Content angles
Any remaining shoot assets — schedule them out over the next 2 weeks so your feed doesn't go dark while you plan your next content batch.
Event recap content (photos, stories, attendee tags) — if your Week 5 event happened, this is your highest-engagement content of the entire plan.
Channels
Google Analytics Meta Ads Manager Email
Tasks
Review your numbers across all channels
Instagram follower growth, email list size, Google review count, ad spend vs. reach, website visits. Write them down in one place. You can't improve what you don't measure.
Measurement Half day
Identify your top-performing content
Which post got the most engagement? Which email had the best open rate? Do more of that. Marketing isn't about doing everything — it's about doubling down on what works.
Measurement Quick
Set a sustainable posting rhythm
Based on what you learned: pick a frequency you can actually maintain. 2 posts and 1 email per week beats 7 posts for one week and then silence for a month.
Content Quick
Decide whether to keep running the Meta ad
If it's bringing people through the door, keep it at the same budget. If not, pause it and redirect that spend to your next content shoot or event.
Measurement Quick
Success signal
You can answer the question "what's working and what should I keep doing?" with specific numbers, not gut feelings.
If not: That's okay — the act of reviewing is the win. Most businesses never look at their data at all. You're already ahead.
