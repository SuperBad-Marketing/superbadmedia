$250/mo avg

Self-serve subscription products for businesses that want the SuperBad operating system without the agency fee. Same Brand DNA engine, same content tooling, automated delivery.

Risk Assessment

What could go wrong,
and what we've done about it.

Outreach Deliverability

Cold email at volume lives or dies on warmup, domain reputation, and targeting precision.

ICP-scoring gate cuts 30–40% of bad-fit prospects before send. LLM-generated copy per prospect (no templates). First 90 days are the calibration window.

Trial-to-Retainer Conversion

25% conversion is a working assumption, not a measured one. If it lands at 15%, services revenue compresses ~40%.

The 6-week plan is the pitch — it demonstrates real value before asking for commitment. SaaS revenue is the structural hedge.

SaaS Ramp Speed

SaaS acquisition depends on paid ads, outreach spillover, content engine, and referrals finding traction simultaneously.

Dedicated ad spend from day 1 ($50/day baseline, scaling to 20% of SaaS revenue when that exceeds the baseline). Content engine is automated (LLM-driven). Conservative scenario still models only 80 subs by Month 12.

Solo Operator Capacity

Andy does the shoots. Retainer count has a human ceiling. Too many clients too fast = quality risk.

Hiring pipeline is sequenced to revenue. Tier limits on SaaS protect margin. Shooter/Editor #1 triples capacity from Month 1. Account Manager at 10 clients.

Platform Completion

SuperBad Lite is ~70% built. Remaining 30% includes key features (Unified Inbox, Content Engine, Lead Generation).

Core revenue features (Sales Pipeline, Quote Builder, Invoicing, SaaS Billing) are already shipped. Remaining waves are growth accelerators, not prerequisites.

Market Competition

Creative agencies exist. Marketing agencies exist. Someone could copy the model.

The moat is the combination: proprietary platform + entertainment-first creative + vertical specialisation + AI-driven operations. Competitors would need to build all four.
